# 2024 ERA unjustified-dismissal determinations

The ERA database search for **“unjustified dismissal”**, dated 1 January to
31 December 2024, returned 130 unique PDFs.  After excluding costs-only,
preliminary/interim, compliance/removal, withdrawn, and non-merits results,
the decision-level baseline is 63 substantive determinations.

| Calculation | Included | Employee wins | Employer wins | Mixed/other | Employee win rate (all) | Binary rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A. Baseline | 63 | 46 | 17 | 0 | 73.0% | 73.0% |
| B. Excluding ERA-confirmed serious misconduct | 61 | 46 | 15 | 0 | 75.4% | 75.4% |
| C. Excluding alleged serious-misconduct dismissals | 52 | 35 | 17 | 0 | 67.3% | 67.3% |
| D. Excluding any s 124 contributory conduct | 61 | 44 | 17 | 0 | 72.1% | 72.1% |

The answer to the research question is therefore **75.4%** on the strict
definition: excluding only cases where the Authority affirmatively found
serious misconduct.  The two removed determinations are [2024] NZERA 319 and
[2024] NZERA 585, both employer wins.

Cases removed from the baseline for B: 319, 585.

Cases removed from the baseline for C: 45, 162, 308, 319, 326, 335, 380, 506,
585, 592, 612, 714, 773.

Cases removed from the baseline for D: 169 (20% contribution), 592 (10%).

The baseline is four percentage points above the EMA/Westlaw 69% figure.  The
most likely explanation is scope/coding: this study uses the supplied public
database query and excludes non-merits follow-ups, while a commercial index can
use different document and aggregation rules.  No result estimates the chance
that an employee who raises a grievance will prevail; confidential settlements,
abandoned claims, and unfiled disputes are outside this corpus.
